02.1 Authoritative implementation
clarifications
A. Planning-buffer warnings W9 Chocolate and W11 Vanilla mean the first relevant breach
AFTER the initial receipt. Opening stock in W1/W2 is already below a two-week buffer in some
cases. Do not call W9 the first-ever breach. Use 'Post-receipt buffer breach' in detail tables. The
initial stock bridge remains visible; never imply all initial buffers were met.

B. 'First unmet requirement Chocolate W11' describes baseline S1. In S6 the first shortfall is W4
across three flavours. In S5 Chocolate first shortfall W10, Vanilla W12. All dynamic copy must
follow the active case.

C. All actual physical stock is nonnegative. Net stock may be negative; label it 'net stock
position'. Negative net is outstanding modelled requirement, not lost sales. Do not sum physical
surplus and backlog into one availability KPI that hides flavour shortages.

D. Safety-stock policy uses strict '<', not '<='. Forecast stress ranges are sensitivity cases, not
probability intervals.

E. Six-week repeat lead time means W3 opening booking -> W9 opening usable receipt. Initial
production was arranged before the snapshot; W1 is a scheduled deposit milestone, not its
six-week booking date. No historical booking date needs to be invented.

F. Manufacturer allows separate flavour repeats is an explicit SYNTHETIC feasibility assumption.
If false, S2 is unavailable; other scenario outputs are not automatically recomputed into a
different optimum. MOQ = 1,200 packs per flavour and increment = 6. Max receipt-slot volume
= 3,600 packs. No live supplier slot is claimed reserved.

G. Forecast quantities are weekly analytical requirements, not individual executable orders. The
lower/higher cases can contain non-case multiples; a case-rounded dispatch schedule is not
modelled. Current explicit orders and proposed production quantities use case multiples. Do not
claim order-level packing feasibility for forecast totals.

H. Scenario presets replace the full active input set. Do not combine independent switches into
uncalculated combinations. Feasibility toggle only tests whether S2 is allowed. Primary option
comparison explicitly compares S1/S2/S3 under central demand and W5 opening, regardless of
a separately inspected stress case.

I. Page 3's 'Preview Chocolate-only repeat' loads S2. 'Compare repeating all three' navigates to
the S1/S2/S3 comparison. Page 4 opening-date choice loads S0, S1 or S7 and resets earlier
changes. Label this reset visibly. Account filter affects historical shipments only.

J. Backlog carry-forward is a modelling convention, not a claim that retailers tolerate late
deliveries. Existing-account vs expansion service allocation would require demand-class backlog
records. The PDF may describe the proposed priority policy, but must not publish account/class
service KPIs without an implemented priority ledger. Aggregate per-SKU backlog outputs below
are canonical.

K. No shelf-life, storage capacity, cash balance, margin, tax, delivery penalties, revenue or
actual savings claim may be inferred. Storage proxy is a comparison based on weekly closing
physical stock, not a vendor invoice; lower cost during shortage is not success.

03 Executive summary and demo
purpose
The central planning question is:

  ANALYTICAL NOTE


  When manufacturing minimums largely determine the first run, how can Fromage
  assess what that stock supports, protect existing retail commitments and prepare
  the next production decision?


The demo must connect demand evidence, forecasts, production constraints, retail expansion
and cash. All pages must use the same underlying records and calculations.

Build five analytical routes around a minimum-led initial run and a prospective ten-store
opening. The default S1 case has 210 Chocolate packs outstanding at W12. S2 adds 1,200
Chocolate packs at W9 for CAD 4,800, clearing that modelled gap through W12. S3 clears it too,
with CAD 10,320 more production commitment and 2,400 more ending packs than S2. S2 is
worth reviewing only if separate flavour runs are feasible.

The demonstration is maintained analysis for discussion. It is neither an approved Fromage plan
nor a system integration. Evidence, constraints, assumptions, stock paths and payments must
remain inspectable.

7.2 Account-level expansion rule
This rule fully defines the underlying 72 account–SKU–week shipment rows.

For each product/week quantity Q:

1. AC-01 = 6 × floor((Q × 0.50) ÷ 6)

2. AC-02 = 6 × floor((Q × 0.30) ÷ 6)

3. AC-03 = Q − AC-01 − AC-02

09 Calculation rules and relationships
Calculation rules
Demand
Total requirement = forecast-consumed existing requirement + opening fill + expansion
recurring requirement

Opening fill and recurring requirements are different records.

Inventory
Use a signed net stock position:

Net closing[t] = Net closing[t−1] + usable receipts[t] − requirement[t]

Then:

• Physical closing = max(0, net closing)

• Outstanding requirement = max(0, −net closing)

Negative values mean unmet requirements, not negative physical stock.

The fixture carries unmet requirements forward as backlog. It does not assume they are
permanently lost sales.

Fulfilment
Available this week = physical opening + usable receipts

Fulfilled = min(available, opening backlog + current requirement)

Carry aggregate backlog forward by SKU. Proposed policy: serve existing backlog, then
existing-account requirements, then prospective expansion. Account/class service allocation is
not calculated; an implemented priority ledger is required before publishing class-level service
KPIs.

Safety target
Safety target = 2 × recurring weekly requirement

Exclude one-time opening fill from this recurring target.

A safety breach occurs when closing stock is strictly below the target. Equality is not a breach.

Weeks of cover
Static cover = physical stock ÷ recurring weekly requirement

Label this static measure separately from the time-phased projection.

Zero demand produces null / no current requirement, not infinity.

Booking deadline
Latest booking date = required usable receipt date − six weeks

Booking deadlines are illustrative and conditional on slot availability.

Inventory value
Closing inventory value = Σ physical closing packs × unit cost

Do not deduct backlog from inventory valuation.

Option comparisons
• Additional production commitment = selected repeat cost.

• Deferred commitment versus another option = difference in production commitments.

• Reduced modelled shortage = difference in outstanding requirements.

• Storage proxy = sum of weekly physical closing stock × CAD 0.04.

Deferred cash commitment is not profit saved.

16 Implementation architecture and
validation gates
Use a single fixture module and pure selectors for demand, receipts, projection, payment
events, option summaries and recommendation state. Store money as integer CAD cents. Keep
raw inputs separate from derived outputs. Use scenario IDs, SKU IDs and source IDs as joins.
Static local data only; no database, authentication, APIs or LLM required to demonstrate the
concept. These are implementation recommendations for Wahib, not product features to
advertise to Fiona.

18 Real-client confirmation checklist

1. The selected three flavours and the actual pack/case configuration.

2. Whether minimums are per flavour, shared batch or full run; allowable increments and
independent-repeat feasibility.

3. Initial-run status, usable receipt dates, payment milestones, usable lead times and genuinely
available supplier slots.

4. Current usable/allocated stock, open orders, recent shipments, stock constraints and any
retailer sell-through.

5. Prospective versus booked openings, opening fills, replenishment assumptions and
commercial delivery expectations.

6. Product costs, taxes, payment terms, usable shelf life, freezer capacity and storage charges.

7. Existing planning tools, data owners, decision rights and a practical weekly refresh process.

8. Suitable forecast horizon, actual seasonality and the period after W12, particularly Vanilla’s
next commitment.

9. Whether an explicit demand-class priority ledger is required before making existing-account
versus expansion service claims.

from this synthetic fixture. The guide supports a reviewable demonstration; commercial
recommendations require actual terms and business context.
